434%

Marketing claim decoded

“434% more testosterone” does not mean testosterone levels rose by 434%

The manufacturer's page cites an eight-week resistance-training study in 57 men. The ashwagandha group had an average testosterone change of 96.2 ng/dL, while the placebo group changed by 18.0 ng/dL.

The “434% more” headline compares the size of those two changes. It does not mean each participant's total testosterone multiplied by more than five times, and the study tested an ashwagandha extract with training — not the entire Testosil formula as a finished product.

Safety first

Natural does not mean risk-free

The manufacturer says there are no reported negative side effects. Independent health sources still recommend caution because ingredients may cause side effects or interact with medications.

01

Ashwagandha

May cause drowsiness, stomach upset, diarrhea or vomiting. Rare liver-injury reports exist. It may interact with diabetes, blood-pressure, thyroid, immune-suppressing, sedative and anti-seizure medicines.

02

Fenugreek

Possible effects include digestive symptoms, allergic reactions and lowered blood sugar. People taking medicines should discuss potential interactions with a health professional.

03

Vitamin and mineral stacking

One serving already supplies the adult upper intake level for zinc and vitamin D. Check every supplement you use and do not assume two servings is appropriate for you simply because it appears on the sales page.

Symptoms are not a diagnosis

If you suspect low testosterone, testing comes before supplements

Fatigue, lower libido, reduced muscle mass, mood changes and erectile problems can have many causes. The Endocrine Society says hypogonadism should be diagnosed only when symptoms are present together with consistently low testosterone concentrations.

Common questions

Testosil FAQ

Short, evidence-aware answers to the questions shoppers most often ask before buying.

Does Testosil work?+

Testosil contains ingredients with varying levels of research. Some evidence is promising for specific standardized extracts, especially ashwagandha, but that does not prove the complete formula will produce the advertised result for every person.

Is Testosil FDA approved?+

No dietary supplement is FDA-approved before sale in the same way a prescription drug is. The manufacturer says the product is made in FDA-registered and inspected facilities; registration of a facility is not approval of the product.

What are the possible Testosil side effects?+

The manufacturer reports no known negative side effects, but ingredients may still cause reactions or interactions. Ashwagandha may cause stomach upset or drowsiness and has rare reports of liver injury. Fenugreek may cause digestive symptoms, affect blood sugar or trigger allergies.

Can Testosil treat clinically low testosterone?+

No. Testosil is a dietary supplement, not an approved treatment for hypogonadism. Men with persistent symptoms should seek medical evaluation and appropriate morning blood testing.

How should Testosil be taken?+

The official label defines one serving as four capsules, and the sales page describes one to two servings daily. One serving already lists 40 mg zinc and 100 mcg vitamin D3, the adult upper intake levels. Ask a clinician or pharmacist whether the labeled use is appropriate for you, especially if you use other supplements.
